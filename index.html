01 Research · 2023

QCross

Cross-platform testing for quantum computing frameworks.

A testing platform that applies differential and metamorphic testing to three quantum SDKs: IBM Qiskit, Rigetti PyQuil and Google Cirq. Run the same program everywhere, transform it in ways that should not change the answer, and look closely when the answers differ.

  • Python
  • Qiskit
  • Cirq
  • PyQuil

Code ↗

Builds on MorphQ, a metamorphic testing approach for Qiskit (arXiv:2206.01111).
02 Language · 2020

lox.js

An interpreter for the Lox programming language, in JavaScript.

A from-scratch implementation of Lox, the small scripting language from Robert Nystrom’s Crafting Interpreters. Scanning, parsing and evaluating, written to understand how a language actually runs.

  • JavaScript
  • Node.js

JSONPath Highlighter

A short syllabus

I’ve worked as an instructor as well as a developer, and I still think the best way to learn a thing is to explain it to someone. Here is my writing arranged as a short course on JavaScript. Every reading is something I wrote.

10 articles, published in Bits and Pieces and codeburst for 2.5K followers on Medium.

  1. Unit 01

    How JavaScript waits

    Callbacks, then promises, then async/await: what an async function returns, where it pauses, and the mistakes that make code accidentally sequential.

    • Deeply Understanding JavaScript Async and Await with Examples Bits and Pieces · May 2019 · 10 min

  2. Unit 02

    Objects, all the way down

    Creating, reading and checking properties; a primer on prototypes; then the hidden attributes (writable, enumerable, configurable) every property carries.

    • Diving Deeper in JavaScript’s Objects Bits and Pieces · Jan 2019 · 9 min

    Metaprogramming

§ 04

About

I’m Arfat. I live in Oslo, which I came to for a Master’s in Computer Science at the University of Oslo. Before that I worked as a full-stack developer and an instructor: building products across the front end and the back end, and teaching other people to do the same.

At work I gravitate towards architecture: where the boundaries go, how data moves through a system, and what will still hold up when the traffic, the codebase and the team are ten times bigger.

Outside of it, I like programming languages and the tools around them: interpreters, paradigms, and the testing that keeps them honest. That curiosity has taken me from Lox to OCaml and Standard ML, and as far as testing the SDKs people use to write quantum programs.
